# Inventory Recommendation: Item 1 Demand Forecast

**Problem.** This memo forecasts daily demand for Item 1, aggregated across all 10 stores, using five years of daily sales history (2013 to 2017). Item 1 was selected because it shows a clean, repeatable weekly and yearly pattern, making it a good representative case for setting reorder policy across the wider catalog. Getting this right matters operationally because under stocking Item 1 means lost sales on the days demand peaks, while over stocking ties up working capital and shelf space that could serve faster moving items.

**Method.** Three approaches were tested against a 90 day held out period: a naive baseline (this day's sales equal the same day last week), a default Prophet model, a Prophet model tuned with explicit weekly and yearly seasonality and a lower changepoint prior scale, and a SARIMA(1,1,1)(1,1,1,7) model. Tuned Prophet came in at an MAE of 15.15 units and MAPE of 7.11 percent, a 20.4 percent improvement in MAE over the naive baseline's 19.03 units (9.07 percent MAPE). The default (untuned) Prophet model actually scored a slightly lower MAE than the tuned version (14.42 vs 15.15), so the tuning mainly bought tighter percentage error and more explicit seasonality control rather than a clear MAE win, worth knowing if this gets revisited. SARIMA underperformed both Prophet variants by a wide margin (MAE 31.27, MAPE 15.84 percent) and was ruled out. Tuned Prophet was carried forward as the forecasting model, and its roughly 7 percent average error is the figure used to size the safety stock buffer below.

**Forecast summary.** The chart below shows actual sales for the trailing 180 days against the tuned Prophet forecast for the next 30 days, with a shaded confidence band. Demand ranges from roughly 195 to 240 units through most weekdays and climbs to the mid 280s to low 290s heading into and through weekends, with a visible upward drift across the 30 day window as the model's seasonal and trend components compound. That weekend peak is the one the business should plan stock around inside this forecast horizon; the wider yearly pattern from the EDA notebook also shows this item running higher through the mid year months, worth building into longer range planning beyond this window.

![30-day forecast](../notebooks/final_forecast_plot.png)

**Recommendation.** Based on this forecast, we recommend triggering reorder at 1,723 units of stock remaining, holding 80 units of safety stock, assuming a 7 day supplier lead time. This is built from an average daily demand of 234.8 units and a demand standard deviation of 18.3 units over the test period, targeting roughly a 95 percent service level (Z = 1.65).

**Caveats.** The tuned Prophet model carries an average error of about 7 percent day to day, so treat the reorder point as a planning figure with some slack either side rather than an exact cutoff. The safety stock calculation assumes a fixed 7 day supplier lead time; if actual lead times run longer or become less reliable, safety stock should be increased accordingly. This model was fit on historical data through late 2017 and should be re-run periodically, ideally monthly, as new sales data comes in, rather than treated as a fixed setting, since any real shift in demand pattern (a promotion, a new competitor, a supply change) will not be reflected until the model is retrained.
